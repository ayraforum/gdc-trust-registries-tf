# The Trust Gap No Standard Can Close: AAMVA, Austroads, and the Governance of Cross-Border mDLs

## Description

ISO/IEC 18013-5 defines the format. It doesn't define who to trust. This session examines how AAMVA and Austroads are tackling exactly that question — building a mutual trust framework for mobile driver's licenses across North America and Australasia. This session grounds the governance concepts introduced in the Day 1 keynote in a live, working example — a real trust framework currently being negotiated across two sovereign regions. The discussion covers the institutional, technical, and governance questions AAMVA and Austroads have had to work through — including timeline, rationale, and how replicable their approach might be elsewhere. A grounded, practitioner-level session for a working group navigating the same challenges at global scale.

## Format

A short framing presentation about the GDC Trust Registries Task Force Track 2 efforts, followed by a panel discussion.

## Relationship to Track 1's Day 2 session

Track 2 wires the answer to a live mobile driver's licence across two sovereign regions. [Track 1's session](../Track1/session-day2-the-pattern-that-repeats.md) works one level up: the same governance function as a pattern that recurs across identity documents, education, professional qualifications, travel, business registration, and trade.

The two sessions share a thesis and split the proof:

- **Track 2** — one credential type, two regions, a real framework under negotiation. Depth.
- **Track 1** — the same missing answer across many domains. Breadth.

Both open on the same gap. Track 2 states it as *"ISO/IEC 18013-5 defines the format. It doesn't define who to trust."* Track 1 generalizes it: formats bind claims to cryptographic evidence, protocols move them, and neither establishes that the actor was authorized.
